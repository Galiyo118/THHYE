Stay Safe Online

Free Internet Safety Resources

Scams and phishing attempts increasingly target older adults. Here are free, trustworthy resources — plus a few quick habits — to help you or a loved one stay safe online.

AARP Fraud Watch Network

Free scam-tracking tools, a helpline, and alerts on the latest scams targeting seniors — no AARP membership required.

aarp.org/money/scams-fraud →

FTC Consumer Advice

The Federal Trade Commission's plain-language guides on recognizing and reporting scams, identity theft, and phishing.

consumer.ftc.gov/features/scam-alerts →

CISA Cybersecurity Basics

The federal Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency's tips for strong passwords, safe browsing, and device security.

cisa.gov/secure-our-world →

National Council on Aging

Guides written specifically for older adults on avoiding fraud and protecting personal and financial information online.

ncoa.org — Scams Targeting Older Adults →

Five Quick Habits That Prevent Most Scams

📞

Hang up and call back

If someone calls claiming to be your bank, the IRS, or a grandchild in trouble, hang up and call the number you already know is real.

🔗

Don't click unexpected links

Unexpected texts or emails about a package, a prize, or a locked account are almost always phishing. Go to the real website directly instead.

🔑

Use a different password everywhere

Reusing one password means one leaked account can unlock all of them. Write passwords down in a notebook if that's easiest to remember.

💳

No gift cards, ever

No real government agency, utility, or business will ever ask you to pay a bill or fine with gift cards. That request always means scam.

👨‍👩‍👧

Ask someone you trust

Before sending money or personal information, pause and ask a family member or call THHYE. A second opinion catches most scams.

🎓

Come to a workshop

Our free sessions walk through real scam examples so you can recognize the warning signs before they cost you anything.

# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
Two audiences, weighted equally:
- **Kids and families** in Central Ohio looking for free, hands-on tech/AI learning (AI literacy, digital citizenship, making games with AI).
- **Seniors and older adults** who want patient, judgment-free help with smartphones, video calls, scam prevention, and social media.
Secondary: community organizations (cultural associations, libraries) deciding whether to host a THHYE workshop.

## Product Purpose
THHYE (Technology Helping Hands for Youth and Elderly) is a student-led nonprofit in the Columbus metro area. High school volunteers teach free technology workshops to kids and older adults. Success: people leave more confident with technology, and organizations ask THHYE back or invite it in.

## Positioning
Students teaching their own community, for free, in person, and tailored to the group: THHYE builds custom workshops for each host organization's audience rather than running one fixed curriculum.

## Operating Context
Workshops happen at libraries (Delaware County District Library Orange Branch, Dublin Public Library), at community organizations' events, and online. Slides, think-pair-share discussion, games/bingo, one-on-one help.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Static HTML/CSS/JS site, hosted at thhye.org (GitHub Pages, CNAME). Google Analytics with cookie consent (common.js).
- Pages: home, online workshops, safety resources, team, partnerships, contact.
- All workshops are free. Custom workshops available for host organizations.

## Brand Commitments
- Name: THHYE, full name "Technology Helping Hands for Youth and Elderly". Logo file THHYELogo.png.
- Founders: Aarav Patel and Aryan Kondekar.
- Visual preference (chosen 2026-11-12): a clean, photo-led nonprofit look, executed straight. Craft bar: Code.org and Khan Academy.
- Reviewers said the current site "looks AI-made": avoid emoji icons and generic icon sets; use custom illustration that shows what THHYE actually does.

## Evidence on Hand
- 100+ people have attended workshops (confirmed by user).
- Partners: GMOCO YUVA Connect, Columbus Maharashtra Mandal (CMM), Telugu Association of Central Ohio (TACO). Logos in assets/.
- Real workshop photos in assets/ (workshop-*.jpg, cmm-photo-*.jpg, taco-photo-*.jpg, online-workshop-*.png, team-*.jpg).
- Real participant testimonials (kids named; elderly anonymized) in index.html.
- Do not invent: workshop counts, ratings, other statistics, extra partners.

## Product Principles
1. Show real people and real rooms over abstract claims.
2. Free and custom: every host gets a workshop built for its people.
3. Two audiences, one mission: neither kids nor seniors are an afterthought.
4. Plain, patient language, the way volunteers actually teach.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Older adults are a core audience: generous text size, high contrast, large tap targets, no reliance on hover or tiny type.
